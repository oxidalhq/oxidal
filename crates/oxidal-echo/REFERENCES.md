# Sources and references — Oxidal Echo

Written for Oxidal from the published descriptions named here and from first
principles. No third-party source code was copied, ported or used as a base,
and no commercial unit was measured or fitted. See
[the clean code rule](../../docs/native-effects.md).

The papers, by the short names used in the table:

- **Tape model** [2]: Chowdhury, "Real-Time Physical Modelling for Analog Tape
  Machines", DAFx-19.
- **Tape delay** [3]: Arnardottir, Abel and Smith, a digital model of a tape
  delay unit, AES Convention 125, October 2008 (its title names the unit, so
  it is not repeated here). Used for its idea only: a tape echo is a delay
  whose length is the tape's travel time between two heads, so speed changes
  bend the pitch. The paper could not be re-read while this was written, and
  nothing was taken from its measurements.
- **Bucket brigade A** [4]: Raffel and Smith, "Practical Modeling of
  Bucket-Brigade Device Circuits", DAFx-10.
- **Bucket brigade B** [5]: Holters and Parker, "A Combined Model for a Bucket
  Brigade Device and its Input and Output Filters", DAFx-18.

| `langevin`, `tape_curve`             | Tape saturation                                                                      | Tape model [2] eq. | Head loss                            | High loss of the playback head                                                       | Tape model [2] §2.3.2: spacing loss `e^(−kd)`, `k = 2πf/v`. Approximated by two equal one-pole low-passes whose joint −3 dB point is the spacing loss's, `f = ln(√2)·v/(2πd)`; two equal poles are 3 dB down at `√(√2 − 1)` of their cutoff. Gap and thickness loss are left out                            | `v` = 19 cm/s, a standard tape speed; `d` = 1 µm when new, ×6 at full Age (×9 in Heads); chosen here                                                                                                              |
| Tape low loss                        | A little bass loss                                                                   | First-order high-pass, textbook                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | 55 Hz, chosen here                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `Bucket`: clock and filters          | Bandwidth that falls as the delay grows                                              | Bucket brigade A [4] §2: delay = `N/(2·f_clock)`; anti-alias and reconstruction filters set between 1/3 and 1/2 of the sampling frequency and as low as 1.5 kHz in echo circuits. Each filter here is one second-order low-pass (`oxidal_dsp::StateVariable`, Q 0.707), not a model of a particular circuit | `N` = 4096 stages; cutoff = clock/3, floor 1.5 kHz (both from the paper's ranges), ceiling 9 kHz chosen here. Below 4.5 kHz the clock stops falling: the model lengthens the line instead                         |
| `Bucket`: sample and hold            | The line takes one sample per clock period and holds it                              | Bucket brigade B [5] §2: the output is the input delayed and convolved with a rectangular pulse one clock period wide. Simplified: computed at the host rate, with a clock edge inside a sample period splitting that period by area, in place of the paper's exact filter evaluation at the clock instants | None                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `Bucket`: compander                  | 2:1 compressor before the line, 1:2 expander after                                   | Bucket brigade A [4] §3.4: expander `avg(abs(x))·x`, feedback compressor `x / avg(abs(f(x)))`                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Reference level 0.1, gain limited to ×4 either way, 2 ms attack and 20 ms release; chosen here                                                                                                                    |
| `Bucket`: noise and saturation       | Hiss that rises as the clock slows; soft limit of the stages                         | Bucket brigade A [4] §3.1 (noise and limited headroom are why the compander is there). Noise is white, taken at each clock edge, scaled by `√(4.5 kHz / clock)` and by Age, and present only while there is signal; the limit is `oxidal_dsp::soft_clip`                                                    | 0.008 of full scale at full Age and the slowest clock; chosen here                                                                                                                                                |
| Bucket colour placed before the line | The whole colour runs on what is written                                             | First principles: every part is time-invariant, so colour then delay equals the same colour split around the delay, for a steady delay time                                                                                                                                                                 | None                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
